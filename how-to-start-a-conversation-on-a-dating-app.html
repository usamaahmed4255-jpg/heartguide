Starting Out

How to Start a Conversation on a Dating App

A first message is not a performance — it's an opening. Here's how to write one that sounds like you and actually gets a reply.

By Jordan Ellis · Starting Out · 11 min read · Last updated 10 November 2026

In this guide

  1. Read the profile first
  2. Open with something specific
  3. Keep it short
  4. Ask a question they can answer
  5. When they reply — and when they don't
  6. Questions people ask

Most first messages fail for a boring reason: they could have been sent to anyone. "Hey", "Hi, how are you?" and "You're gorgeous" all land the same way — like a knock on the door with nobody standing behind it. The person on the other side has no idea who you are, what you noticed, or why you picked them out of a hundred other profiles. So they scroll on.

A good opener does three small things. It shows you actually looked. It gives the other person something easy to respond to. And it sounds like a real person wrote it, not a script. You do not need to be clever or funny on demand. You need to be specific, warm, and brief. That is the whole job.

Read the profile first

Before you type a word, read the whole profile. Not just the first photo. The bio, the prompts, the photos in order, the little details — the dog in the third picture, the book on the bedside table, the marathon medal, the badly made pizza they are proud of. Most people put something in there on purpose. Your job is to find it.

This takes about thirty seconds, and it changes everything. A message that mentions a real detail feels completely different from a message that could go to anyone. It tells the other person you were paying attention, which is, honestly, half of what dating is. It also saves you time. If their profile says they hate hiking and your perfect weekend is a six-hour walk in the hills, you have learned something useful before either of you invested an evening in small talk.

Open with something specific

Specific beats smooth. A line that references one real thing in their profile will outperform a polished compliment every time, because it cannot be copy-pasted to the next ten people. You are not trying to impress them with wordplay. You are trying to start a conversation that only the two of you could have.

Pick one detail and react to it honestly. If they have a photo cooking, ask what was in the pan. If they mention a favourite film, tell them the scene you still think about. If they play an instrument, ask what they are learning at the moment. Keep your tone light and curious, not intense. You are opening a door, not proposing.

"Okay, I need to know — is that pizza in your third photo homemade, or are you just very loyal to one place in Brooklyn?"

Keep it short

Two or three sentences is plenty. Four at the absolute most. A first message is not the place for your life story, your dating history, or a paragraph explaining why you swiped right. Long messages feel heavy before a conversation has even started, and they put pressure on the other person to match your effort in reply.

Short does not mean lazy. "Hey" is short and says nothing. A good short message says one specific thing and asks one easy question. Think of it like walking up to someone at a party. You would not launch into a monologue. You would say something small, see how they respond, and go from there.

Ask a question they can answer

A question is what turns a comment into a conversation. Without one, even a nice message can stall, because the other person has to invent a reply from nothing. With one, they have a clear next step. Make it easy. The best first-message questions are ones the person can answer in a sentence, without thinking too hard.

  • About a photo: "Where was that hike?" is easier than "Tell me about yourself."
  • About a hobby: "What are you reading at the moment?" beats "Do you like books?"
  • About a choice: "Beach holiday or city break — pick one" gives them something fun to answer.
  • About food: "Best coffee near you, go" works because everyone has an opinion.
  • About a prompt: If they answered a prompt, ask about the answer, not the prompt itself.

When they reply — and when they don't

When they reply, match their energy. If they send two sentences, do not answer with ten. If they ask you a question, answer it, then ask one back. A conversation is a rally, not a speech. The aim in the first few exchanges is simple: find one thing you both enjoy talking about, and stay there for a while.

Do not rush to move off the app. A few good exchanges first, then suggest a low-key meet — coffee on a Saturday morning, a walk, a drink after work. Something short, public, and easy to say yes to. If the chat is going well, the suggestion will feel natural. If it is not, no amount of clever timing will fix it.

And when they do not reply, let it go. No reply is also an answer, and it is rarely about you. They might be busy, talking to someone else, or off the app for a while. One gentle follow-up after a day or two is fine — something light, like a comment on a photo you missed. After that, move on. Double-texting a stranger, or sending a message asking why they did not reply, never helps. It just makes the next person you message carry the weight of the last one.

Questions people ask

Is "hey" really that bad as an opener? It is not rude, it is just empty. It gives the other person nothing to work with, and on a busy app it blends in with twenty other identical messages. One specific sentence will always do more work than a polite greeting on its own.

Should I compliment their looks in the first message? A light, genuine compliment is fine — "great smile" or "you look really happy in that photo" — but leading with looks alone can feel shallow, especially if it is the only thing you noticed. A compliment about something they chose, like a photo or a hobby, usually lands better.

How long should I wait before sending a follow-up? Give it at least a day. People check apps at odd hours, and a message sent at 11pm might not be seen until the next evening. One friendly follow-up is enough. If there is still no reply, take it as a no and keep going.

Dirty Talk for Beginners

Dirty talk feels ridiculous until it doesn’t. How to start small, find words that feel like yours, and stop when it stops being fun.

By Jordan Ellis · Deeper In · 11 min read · Last updated 11 November 2026

  2. Small words first
  3. Say what you see
  4. When it feels silly
  5. Words to avoid

Dirty talk sounds easy until you try it. Then your brain goes blank, or the words come out wrong, or you start laughing, or you say something that sounded great in your head and terrible out loud. Everyone has been there. The people who are good at it were not born that way. They started small, felt silly, and kept going anyway.

The secret is that dirty talk is not about being filthy. It is about saying what you feel, out loud, in the moment. Most of it is just honest sentences said at the right time. You do not need a vocabulary lesson. You need permission — from yourself, and from your partner — to sound like you.

Start outside the bedroom

The easiest place to start is not in bed. It is in normal life. A text in the middle of the day. “I keep thinking about last night.” A quiet word when you get home. “You look good today.” These are not dirty talk yet, but they are the same muscle. You are saying something a little bit bold, out loud, to the person you want. Once that feels normal, the bedroom version is a much smaller step.

Starting outside the bedroom also takes the pressure off. In bed, everything feels like a performance. In the kitchen, it is just a sentence. And when your partner responds well to a small bold sentence during the day, you will know the door is open — which makes the next step much easier.

Small words first

Do not open with a whole speech. Start with one or two words. “Yes.” “More.” “Like that.” “Don’t stop.” These are real dirty talk, and they are the most powerful kind, because they are honest and they are yours. You do not need to sound like anyone else. The goal is not to impress your partner with your vocabulary. The goal is to let them know what you are feeling.

Try this: “You look so good right now.” “I love this.” “Don’t stop.” Three sentences. That is a whole vocabulary to start with.

As you get comfortable, the sentences get longer on their own. You will find your own words — the ones that feel natural coming out of your mouth. Some people are sweet. Some people are blunt. Some people are funny. All of those work. The only kind that does not work is the kind that sounds like someone else.

Say what you see

The easiest dirty talk of all is just describing what is happening. “I love the way you look right now.” “You feel so good.” “I love it when you do that.” You are not inventing anything. You are just saying what you notice, out loud. It works because it is true, and because hearing what you are feeling makes your partner feel wanted in a way that silence never does.

Being specific helps. “You are amazing” is fine. “The way you just moved — do that again” is better, because it could only be said right now, to this person. Specific is always better than generic. And if you run out of words, just say what you feel in your body. “I can’t get enough of this.” That is enough. That is more than enough.

When it feels silly

It will feel silly. That is not a sign you are doing it wrong. That is just what it feels like at first. The trick is not to let the silliness stop you. If you both laugh, laugh together and keep going. Laughing in bed is not a failure. It is a good sign — it means you are comfortable enough to be ridiculous with each other. The couples who laugh during sex are usually the ones having the best time.

If the silliness is too much and you freeze, that is fine too. Stop talking. Go back to touch. Try again another time. Nobody is keeping score. The point is to have fun together, not to deliver a performance. The moment it stops being fun, stop doing it. That is the only rule that matters.

Words to avoid

Not every word works for every person, and some words are a hard no for a lot of people. The only way to know is to ask. “Is there anything you don’t like hearing?” is a good question, and it is much easier to ask it outside the bedroom than in the middle of things. Some people hate being called certain names. Some people hate swearing. Some people love it. There is no universal list.

  • Ask what words they hate — and believe the answer.
  • If a word makes them flinch, drop it. Do not test it twice.
  • Never use words about them that you would not want used about you.
  • When in doubt, use their name. It is simple, and it always works.

The rule is simple: the words belong to both of you. If one of you does not like a word, it is gone. That is not a restriction. It is respect. And respect is what makes the rest of it fun.

Questions people ask

What if I freeze and can’t think of anything? Then say nothing. Touch is also communication. A kiss, a held hand, a sound — all of it counts. The words will come back when you are relaxed. Freezing is not failing. It is just your brain catching up.

What if they laugh at me? They are probably laughing with you, not at you. Dirty talk is inherently a bit funny, especially at first. If they are laughing kindly, laugh too. If they are actually mocking you, that is a different problem, and it is not about the words.

Do I have to swear? No. Plenty of people never swear during sex and have a wonderful time. Dirty talk is about honesty, not swearing. “I want you” is dirty talk. So is “you feel amazing.” Use the words that feel like yours.

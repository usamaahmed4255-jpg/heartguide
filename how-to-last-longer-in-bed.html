How to Last Longer in Bed

Finishing too fast is common, fixable, and nothing to be ashamed of. Practical things that actually help — no pills, no gimmicks.

By Jordan Ellis · Deeper In · 11 min read · Last updated 11 November 2026

  4. Change the rhythm

If you finish faster than you would like, you are in very large company. It happens to most men at some point, and to plenty of men most of the time. Nobody talks about it, which makes it feel like a secret shame instead of what it actually is: a common, fixable thing. The silence around it is the worst part. It turns a small problem into a big worry, and the worry makes it worse.

The good news is that lasting longer is mostly a skill, not a talent. It is about slowing down, paying attention to your body, and having a few simple techniques ready. No pills, no gimmicks, no expensive gadgets. Just practical things that work for most people, most of the time.

It is more common than you think

First, the facts, because they help. Most men last somewhere between three and seven minutes once sex starts. That is normal. The idea that everyone else lasts half an hour comes from films, and films are lying. If you finish in a couple of minutes and it bothers you, that is worth working on — but it does not mean something is wrong with you. It means your body is doing what bodies do when they are excited.

The worry is usually worse than the problem. Men who finish fast often spend the whole time thinking “don’t finish, don’t finish,” which is the worst possible thought, because it keeps all your attention on exactly the thing you are trying not to do. Relaxing about it is not just nice advice. It is part of the fix. The calmer you are, the longer you last. That is how bodies work.

If it happens most times and it genuinely bothers you both, a doctor can help — that is normal, not embarrassing. There are real treatments, and asking about them is no different from asking about anything else.

Slow down before you need to

The mistake most men make is waiting until they are nearly there, then trying to slam on the brakes. By then it is too late. The trick is to slow down early, before you feel close. When things start to feel intense, ease off. Go slower. Stop moving for a few seconds and kiss them instead. Change what you are doing for a moment. You are not stopping the fun. You are stretching it out.

Breathing helps more than people expect. Slow, deep breaths — in through your nose, out through your mouth — calm your whole body down, and a calm body lasts longer. Fast, shallow breathing does the opposite. It sounds too simple to work, and that is why most people never try it. Try it. It works.

The pause that works

There is a simple technique that sex therapists have recommended for decades, and it is exactly as boring as it sounds — which is why it works. When you feel close to finishing, stop. Just stop moving, take a breath, and wait for the feeling to pass. It usually takes ten or twenty seconds. Then start again, slower. Do it as many times as you need to. Your partner will not mind the pause. Most of the time they will not even notice it, or they will enjoy the slow moments in between.

The key is catching it early. If you wait until you are right at the edge, stopping does not help — you are already past the point. Learn what “getting close” feels like in your body, and pause at the first sign, not the last. It takes a few tries to get the timing right. That is normal. You are learning a skill, and skills take practice.

Change the rhythm

If one rhythm gets you there too fast, change it. Slow down, speed up, stop, start again. Switch to something gentler for a while. Variety is not just more interesting — it is a practical tool. Your body gets used to one steady rhythm and finishes on schedule. A changing rhythm keeps it guessing, and that buys you time.

This is also where being a good partner helps you. Paying attention to them — what they like, what makes them respond — takes your focus off your own countdown. The more your attention is on them, the less you are stuck in your own head thinking about finishing. It is the same advice as before, from the other side: attention beats anxiety, every time.

What to do after

So you finished faster than you wanted. It happens. What you do next matters more than what just happened. Do not apologise five times. Do not go quiet and stare at the ceiling. Do not make it a big deal, because the bigger a deal you make of it, the bigger it becomes in both your heads. Stay close. Hold them. Kiss them. Ask if they are all right. And remember that sex does not have to end when you finish. There is plenty you can still do — with your hands, your mouth, your attention — and most partners would much rather have that than an apology.

And sometimes there is a second round. After a short rest, a lot of men last much longer the second time. It is not a failure to need a pause. It is just how bodies work. The goal was never to be perfect. The goal was for both of you to enjoy yourselves, and there are many ways to get there.

Questions people ask

Do numbing sprays and creams work? They can help, but they are a shortcut, not a fix. They dull the feeling, which can make sex less enjoyable for both of you, and they can transfer to your partner. Try the techniques here first. If you do try a spray, use the smallest amount, and talk to your partner about it first.

How long is normal? A few minutes is normal. The average is somewhere between three and seven minutes. If you are in that range and your partner is happy, there is nothing to fix. The only number that matters is whether both of you are satisfied.

Is it her fault? No. And thinking that way will make it worse, because it puts pressure on her to fix something that is not hers to fix. This is your body, your timing, and your skill to build. Blame has no place in it.

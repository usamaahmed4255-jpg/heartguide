How to Spice Up a Long-Term Relationship in the Bedroom

Years in, the sex gets predictable — not because the spark died, but because the routine took over. Small changes that actually bring it back.

By Jordan Ellis · Deeper In · 11 min read · Last updated 11 November 2026

In this guide

  2. Change one thing at a time
  3. Flirt like you used to
  4. Plan it (yes, really)
  5. Talk about the boring parts
  6. Questions people ask

After a few years together, most couples end up having the same sex, in the same place, at the same time, in the same order. It is comfortable. It is also, after a while, a bit boring. And then someone starts to wonder if the spark has died. It has not died. It has just been buried under routine. The person is still the person you wanted. The routine is the problem, and routines can change.

Spicing things up does not mean doing something wild. It does not mean a whole new personality, or a drawer full of things you bought in a panic. It means small changes, made on purpose, one at a time. Here is how that actually works.

It’s the routine, not the person

The first thing to get straight is that predictable sex is not a sign you picked the wrong person. It is a sign you have been together long enough to build habits, and habits are what long relationships are made of. You eat the same breakfast. You sit in the same seats. You have the same sex. That is normal. The problem is not the person lying next to you. The problem is that nobody ever interrupts the pattern on purpose.

So stop blaming the spark, and stop blaming each other. The spark was never a magic thing that lives or dies on its own. It is what happens when two people pay attention to each other. When you stop paying attention, it fades. When you start again, it comes back. That is good news, because attention is something you can choose to give.

Change one thing at a time

The mistake people make is trying to change everything at once. New place, new time, new everything, all in one night — and then it feels forced, and they decide spicing things up does not work. Change one thing. Just one. A different room. A different time of day. Music on instead of silence. One small change is enough to make the whole thing feel new, and it does not feel like a performance.

Pick one thing to change this week. Just one. A different room, a different time, the lights on instead of off. One change is enough.

Next week, change something else. That is how a routine gets rebuilt instead of just interrupted once. Small changes, stacked up over a month, turn into a sex life that feels different — without anyone having to become a different person.

Flirt like you used to

Remember the early days, when a text in the middle of the day could change your whole mood? That was not magic either. It was flirting, and most couples stop doing it somewhere around year three. They start treating each other like roommates who happen to share a bed. The fix is embarrassingly simple: start flirting again. A text that says “thinking about you” instead of “don’t forget the milk.” A look across the room that means something. A hand on their back as you walk past.

Flirting during the day changes the evening. It builds a little tension, a little anticipation, and by the time you are in bed together, you are already halfway there. It costs nothing. It takes ten seconds. And it is the single most underrated thing in a long relationship.

Plan it (yes, really)

Planning sex sounds like the least romantic thing in the world. It is not. Waiting for it to happen on its own is how couples go weeks without it. Life is busy. Work is tiring. The kids, the house, the endless list of things to do. If you do not plan it, it does not happen. A plan is not unromantic. A plan is a promise.

  • Pick a night and protect it. No phones, no chores, no “just one more episode.”
  • Make it a date, not an appointment. Cook together, or order in, or go out first.
  • Build in something to look forward to — a bath, a massage, a bottle of something you both like.
  • If the plan gets interrupted, do not give up on the week. Move it, do not cancel it.

The couples who plan it are not less spontaneous. They are just honest about how life works. Spontaneity is lovely when it happens. A plan is what makes sure something happens at all.

Talk about the boring parts

A lot of the time, the bedroom is not the problem. The problem is everything around it. One of you is exhausted. The other is stressed. The house is a mess, the bills are late, and nobody has had a proper night’s sleep in weeks. You cannot fix the bedroom without talking about the boring parts, because the boring parts are what is draining you both.

So talk about them. Not in bed, not as a complaint — just honestly, over coffee or on a walk. “I am too tired most nights. What can we change?” Sometimes the answer is practical: an earlier night, a chore shared differently, a weekend with nothing planned. Fixing the tiredness fixes more than the tiredness. It gives you both your evenings back.

Questions people ask

What if only one of us wants to change things? Then start with that one person changing one small thing, and see what happens. Enthusiasm is catching. But do not force it. If your partner is happy and you are bored, say so honestly — “I want more fun for us” is a much better sentence than “you are boring.” And listen to their answer. They might be bored too and just waiting for someone to say it first.

Is it normal to want less sex after years together? Yes. Wanting it less often is normal. Wanting it never, and never talking about it, is the part to watch. Frequency changes. What matters is that both of you are honest about it, and that neither of you is quietly resenting the other.

Do toys help? They can, if both of you are open to it. They are not a replacement for anything. They are just another way to have fun together. If one of you is curious and the other is unsure, start small, talk about it first, and go at the slower person’s pace. No pressure, no big purchase, just a conversation.

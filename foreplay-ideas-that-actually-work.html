Foreplay Ideas That Actually Work

Foreplay is not a warm-up act — for most women it is the main event. Here is what actually works, without the cheesy scripts.

By Jordan Ellis · Deeper In · 11 min read · Last updated 11 November 2026

In this guide

  2. Touch that isn’t about sex
  3. Words count too
  4. Change the place
  5. Ask what they want

Ask most women what the best part of sex is and they will not say the last two minutes. They will say the twenty minutes before it. Foreplay is not the boring bit you rush through to get to the real thing. For a lot of people, it is the real thing. The problem is that most of what people are taught about foreplay comes from films, where it lasts about forty seconds and looks effortless. Real foreplay is slower, messier, and much better than that.

The good news is that good foreplay is not complicated. It does not need props, costumes, or a script. It needs time, attention, and a bit of curiosity about the person in front of you. Here is what actually works, in plain terms.

Slow down on purpose

The single biggest foreplay mistake is speed. Most people rush because they are nervous, or excited, or they think taking time means they are doing it wrong. It does not. Slowing down is not a delay. It is the whole point. When you slow down, you notice things — what makes them breathe differently, what makes them lean in, what makes them laugh. That is information you cannot get at full speed.

Try this: give yourself twice as long as you think you need. If you usually spend five minutes, spend ten. It will feel strange at first, like you are doing nothing. You are not doing nothing. You are paying attention, and attention is the most attractive thing in the bedroom. The people who are best at this are not the ones with the most moves. They are the ones who are not in a hurry.

Touch that isn’t about sex

The best foreplay often starts long before the bedroom, and it does not look like foreplay at all. A hand on the back while they cook. A slow kiss when you get home that does not immediately lead anywhere. Sitting close on the couch with your hand on their leg. Touch that asks for nothing is the most relaxing thing in the world, and relaxed people enjoy sex more. When every touch is a signal that sex is about to happen, touch starts to feel like pressure. When touch is just touch, it feels like being wanted.

Try this: kiss them like you mean it, not like you are in a hurry to get somewhere. A proper kiss, slow, with no next step planned.

In bed, the same rule holds. Start somewhere that is not the obvious place. Their neck. Their shoulders. The inside of their wrist. Take your time getting anywhere. The build-up is what makes the rest of it good — and if you skip it, you are skipping the part most people remember.

Words count too

Foreplay is not only hands. Words do a lot of the work, and most people barely use them. Not a speech. Just noticing out loud. “You look good tonight” lands harder than any technique. “I have been thinking about you all day” changes the whole mood of a room. People want to feel wanted, and the fastest way to show that is to say it, plainly, in your own words.

You do not need to be smooth. You need to be specific. “You are beautiful” is nice. “I love the way you laugh when I do that” is better, because it could only be said to them. The more specific you are, the more it means. And if words feel awkward, start small. One honest sentence is worth more than a whole performance of someone else’s lines.

Change the place

The bedroom is fine, but it is not the only room in the house. A change of place wakes everything up, and it does not have to be dramatic. The shower together, slow, no rush. The kitchen, after dinner, when the dishes can wait. The couch, with the lights low. Different places make you pay attention differently, and that attention is what makes it good.

  • The shower: warm water, slow, no plan. Just the two of you and nowhere to be.
  • The kitchen: after dinner, leaning against the counter, a long kiss that nobody planned.
  • The couch: lights low, phone in another room, nowhere to rush to.
  • The morning: no alarm, no schedule, just the two of you waking up slowly.
  • Outside the house: a walk that turns into something more when you get home.

None of this needs planning or props. It needs a small break from the routine. The routine is the enemy, not the bedroom.

Ask what they want

Here is the simplest foreplay idea there is, and the one people skip most often: ask. “What do you want right now?” is a better question than any list of ideas. Your partner knows what they like. They have probably been waiting for someone to ask. And the answer is usually simpler than you expect. Slower. Softer. Longer. That bit again. People are not mysterious about this. They are just rarely asked.

Asking does not ruin the mood. It makes the mood. It says you care about what they actually enjoy, not about performing a routine you learned somewhere. And once you know, you can do it again next time — which is how good sex gets better instead of just repeating itself.

Questions people ask

How long should foreplay last? There is no number. Twenty minutes is a good place to start for most couples, and longer is usually better than shorter. The honest answer is: until they are ready, not until you are bored. Watch for the signs — breathing, movement, the way they hold you. They will tell you.

What if one of us wants to skip it? Then say so, and say why. Sometimes you are both in a hurry and that is fine — quick sex has its place. But if one of you always wants to skip it and the other always wants it, that is a conversation, not a compromise you make silently every time.

Does foreplay have to lead to sex? No. Sometimes it leads to falling asleep in each other’s arms, and that is a good evening. When foreplay always has to end in sex, it starts to feel like a transaction. When it can just be closeness, it stays fun.
